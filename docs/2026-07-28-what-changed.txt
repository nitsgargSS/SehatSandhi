================================================================================
SEHATSANDHI — WHAT CHANGED ON 28 JULY 2026
A plain summary for reading. 38 commits, 17 database migrations, both projects.
================================================================================

Roughly half of today was building what you asked for. The other half was
finding things that were already broken and had no way of announcing themselves
— a doctor's speciality that was collected and thrown away, a login that did not
exist, a counter that under-reported. Those are called out as they come up,
because they are the ones you could not have seen from the outside.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PRICING IS NOW ONE NUMBER YOU CONTROL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You said the ₹1,000 felt hardcoded and that you wanted a free hand. You were
right, and it was worse than it looked.

The price was written into the plan's NAME ("Launch offer — ₹1,000/month") and
into its permanent CODE ("launch_1000"). The website printed the name as its
headline and the real number just below it, so changing the price in admin gave
two different prices on one screen — with the stale one in larger type.

There was a second problem you had not seen. The plan carried a cap of 50
signups, and the queue advances by itself when a cap fills. The 51st business
would have been quoted ₹2,000 with nobody deciding that. That is the opposite of
a free hand.

Now: prices live in one editable field in Admin → Billing. Names and codes cannot
contain an amount — the database refuses it. All seat caps are cleared, so
nothing moves the price except someone typing one. The change reaches the
website, the vertical pages and the signup wizard with no deploy.

The term also stopped being forced. Checkout used to preselect five months and
the landing page advertised the five-month total as though it were the offer. It
now opens at one month and the business picks.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. GST IS LIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Configured on both projects: NG Technologies, 06AELPG4279G1ZD, Haryana, 18%,
SAC 998365. Invoices are numbered gaplessly per financial year.

The GSTIN you first sent began "01", which is the Jammu & Kashmir state code. The
last character of a GSTIN is a checksum over the rest, and it only balances for
"06" — so the number was not merely inconsistent with a Haryana address, it was
not a valid GSTIN at all. Now checked automatically on both our number and any
customer's.

A business can enter its own GSTIN and claim the 18% back as input credit.
Nothing had ever asked for it: the field existed in the database and in the tax
maths, but no input was ever drawn on screen, so every invoice would have gone
out with no recipient GSTIN. It also meant inter-state was unreachable — a Punjab
customer would have been charged CGST+SGST on an inter-state supply, which is
wrong tax rather than a missing convenience.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ADMIN LOGIN WAS NOT REAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The admin password was compiled into the public JavaScript. "admin@SS2026" sat in
the file every visitor downloads, next to the "hidden" admin URL. Anyone could
read both. Treat that password as compromised; it no longer works anywhere.

Admin is now a real Supabase account with a server-side password, and there is a
change-password screen that asks for the current one first — so an open tab
cannot be used to lock you out.

The same work uncovered a bug that would have bitten on your first registration:
the approval queue could not see pending doctors at all, and Activate would have
written nothing. The dashboard read with the public key, which is only permitted
to see ACTIVE listings. Nobody had noticed because there were no doctors yet.

The same fault turned out to exist for camps, and for an organisation's
specialities and subscriptions. Found by auditing all 47 tables. Worth knowing
why it hid: an empty table and a forbidden table both return nothing, so
"no rows" proved nothing until rows existed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE ADMIN PANEL WORKS ON A PHONE NOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The sidebar was 224 pixels wide, fixed, and sat on top of the content while also
pushing it 224 pixels right — leaving about 70 pixels of usable width on a phone.
Every tab was unreadable, not only the new ones.

Doctors, Camps and Organisations are now cards with the action buttons in reach.
Approve had been off the right edge behind a sideways scroll with nothing to
indicate it existed. Other tables still scroll sideways but now say so.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. REPORTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There was no data behind it. A table for site visits existed in the database and
had never been written to, so any report would have been a chart of zero.
Recording started today — searches, listing impressions, profile opens, WhatsApp
taps, signup starts. History cannot be backfilled, which is why it shipped before
the dashboards.

Nothing personal is stored: no IP, no location, no device fingerprint. The
session id lives only for the browser tab, so two visits on different days cannot
be linked to each other.

A clinic sees its own funnel — appeared in search, profile opened, WhatsApp
tapped, booked — plus what became of those appointments. The ratio is the point:
"seen 2,418 times, opened 312" tells a clinic its photo is the problem, not its
price.

You see the platform, plus one view built for your expansion question: what
patients searched for against whether anyone was listed to serve them. Searches
you could not answer are drawn in amber. That is the argument for entering a town.

Expect near-empty charts on production for a while — recording started today
against a site with no traffic.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. LOCATIONS AND HOURLY WINDOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A doctor sitting in two places could not say so — a listing had one address, so
"Mon–Wed Jagadhri, Thu–Sat Radaur" was inexpressible and a patient was never told
which building to walk into. Locations are now their own thing, managed by the
doctor, free in any pincode as you chose.

Hourly windows were blocked outright by a database rule allowing exactly one
appointment per doctor per instant. Correct for a 15-minute slot; fatal for a
12–1 window, where the second patient's booking simply fails. It is replaced by a
capacity the clinic sets per location — your choice, so a busy OPD can take eight
an hour where a specialist takes three.

One thing this needs and does not have: the WhatsApp bot is a separate backend
and has not been updated. Bookings still work and default to the main location,
but until the bot asks "which clinic?" patients cannot choose one or see hourly
windows.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. HOSPITALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A hospital registered as one anonymous listing, its doctors were invisible, and
it paid a single GP's price. The database had the structure for organisations and
their doctors from the very beginning, and nothing in the app had ever written to
it.

A hospital now lists its consultants during signup and manages them afterwards.
Each becomes a real listing — own profile, own calendar, findable by speciality —
so they inherit everything else with no special casing.

Pricing changed twice today at your direction. It began as "three included, then
₹300 each", which is why three doctors quoted ₹1,000 when you tested. It is now
₹1,000 per doctor: three doctors, ₹3,000. Both models remain available as a
dropdown in admin, because that is a commercial decision, not a code change.

There was a real bug in between. On the pricing step the listing does not exist
yet, so the server counted zero consultants and returned one doctor's price —
overriding the local figure that had counted them correctly. At payment the real
listing is passed and the headcount reappears. You would have been shown ₹1,180
and charged ₹2,360. Quoting one price and taking another is the worst version of
a pricing fault, and it was live.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SPECIALITY WAS BEING THROWN AWAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the one worth dwelling on.

"Category / speciality" in the signup form was free text, and the code that
created the listing never read it. Every doctor was stored as "GEN" regardless of
what they practise. Patients search on an exact speciality match, so a
cardiologist registering today would never have appeared in a cardiology search
— and neither of you would have had any way to notice.

It is now a dropdown, it is saved, and it is required. There is also a way to
correct it in admin, since anyone registered before today is stored wrongly.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. CLINICS CAN LOG IN AT ALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You asked how a doctor logs in. The answer was that they could not.

There are two signup paths. The old form creates an account with a password. The
wizard — the one we have been building, the one that takes payment — creates a
listing and no account whatsoever. So a business could sign up, pay ₹1,180, be
activated, and have no way to reach the dashboard where its reports, roster,
GSTIN and appointments live. Production had zero non-admin accounts.

Login is now a six-digit code to the WhatsApp number, which is required at signup
and is what this audience expects. An unknown number gets the same reply as a
known one, so the form cannot be used to discover which numbers are businesses.
Codes are stored hashed, single use, ten-minute expiry, five wrong tries, and a
sixty-second resend cooldown — without which it is a free SMS cannon pointed at
any number we hold and billed to us.

One login reaches every listing on that number, so a doctor who also runs a
pharmacy sees both.

Codes cannot actually be delivered yet — no WhatsApp or SMS provider is
configured — so sandbox shows the code on screen instead.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. THE CLINIC DASHBOARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Six tabs: Today, Appointments, Schedule, Clinic, Bills, Reports.

Appointments and Bills are new. Before today a clinic could see its twenty most
recent bookings and nothing else — no history, no patient search, no view of next
week. Worse, the "This month" figure was counted from those same twenty rows, so
it silently under-reported for any clinic past its twentieth booking. A wrong
number presented as fact is worse than a missing screen.

Bills lists every invoice with the tax split spelled out, because that is the
number a business hands its accountant to claim the credit. Until today the only
copy was a WhatsApp link sent once at payment.

Different categories now see different tabs. A pharmacy has no appointment book,
so it was being shown today's consultation slots and two permanent zeroes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. PHARMACY, INSURANCE AND AMBULANCE PAY NOTHING MONTHLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

As you decided today, the flat fee applies to doctors, hospitals and labs only.
The other three are free to list and owe 10% of billing — which is what their
pages have always promised. Before this change they were being charged ₹1,000 a
month while their own pages said "no monthly fee, and if nothing sells you pay
nothing".

Their signup takes no payment and the wizard shows no Pay button; an attempt to
charge them is refused by the server.

Two things follow from this. The 10% is a term, not something we can bill — we
can see a WhatsApp tap but not what a pharmacy dispensed, so until they report
their billing these are free listings. And because there is no payment, their
listing stays pending until you approve it, which is the intended gate for drug
licence and IRDA checks.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. PAYMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Razorpay works. A real test payment went end to end: ₹3,540 charged, listing
activated, price and term locked for the period paid, invoice SS/2026-27/0005
issued, and the arithmetic reconciling exactly.

A few things had to be fixed to get there. Checkout was offering to save the
card, which adds a second consent screen with its own OTP — that is the screen
you got stuck on. The phone number was passed to Razorpay with "+91 " and spaces
still in it, which their form does not accept. And the invoice link opened in a
new tab, where the sandbox choice was lost, so a valid invoice reported itself as
invalid.

A webhook now exists, and it matters more than it sounds. Before it, the entire
post-payment chain depended on the customer's browser returning from Checkout. If
their phone died or they closed the tab, Razorpay had the money and we had no
record: payment stuck pending, listing never activated, no invoice, and you would
find out when they complained. Every other failure here is recoverable by
retrying. That one takes money and leaves nothing to retry from.

Still untested: closing the tab mid-payment. A normal payment fires both paths,
so it does not exercise the webhook's reason for existing.

Production has no Razorpay keys and no webhook yet. Live keys need their own.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. SITE SPEED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every page shipped in one 865 kB bundle, so a patient opening the homepage from a
WhatsApp link downloaded the admin dashboard, the billing screens and the signup
wizard before seeing anything. Now 496 kB — 43% less, and it matters on a rural
connection.
